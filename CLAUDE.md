# Half-Life movement for Clair Obscur: Expedition 33

A UE4SS Lua mod that gives Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 a first-person camera and Half-Life 1 movement (bunny hopping by holding jump, air strafing, Half-Life's ducking and OpenAG's +ducktap) while exploring.

## Layout

- `HL1Movement/`: the folder players copy into `...\Sandfall\Binaries\Win64\ue4ss\Mods\`. `enabled.txt` makes UE4SS load it without editing `mods.txt`.
  - `Scripts/main.lua`: entry point. Settings, hotkeys, hooks, and the per-frame checks of when the mod may act.
  - `Scripts/config.lua`: player settings, in Half-Life units.
  - `Scripts/hl1movement/`: `physics.lua`, `duck.lua` (Half-Life's duck and OpenAG's duck button, per update) and `settings.lua` (pure Lua), `movement.lua`, `hull.lua` (Unreal's crouch as the ducked hull, the duck tap's lift, the floor below), `keys.lua` (the mod's keys taken from the game, the hotkeys' binds), `view.lua` (which camera the game shows, and when first person may be on), `camera.lua`, `input.lua`, `overrides.lua` (remembers and restores game values), `journal.lua` (the list of changes that survives a UE4SS mods restart), `guard.lua` (runs each step on its own and logs its errors), `ue.lua` (safe UE4SS helpers), `log.lua`, `diagnostics.lua` (the report), `speedometer.lua` (the speedometer, a UMG widget).
- `tests/`: `lua5.4 tests/run.lua`. `fake_game.lua` stands in for UE4SS and the game, closely enough to run `main.lua` frame by frame, with E33's camera set-up (several cameras on the character; Unreal shows the first active one) and Unreal's falling, walking and landing (see "Slopes", the fake game's ground). Its extension points (`fake.extensions`, held by `tests/test_fake_game.lua`) carry `tests/fake_input.lua` (Enhanced Input: mapping contexts, the game's walk, run and jump actions on the mod's keys, the wheel's notches) and `tests/fake_hull.lua` (Unreal's crouch as the 0.4.0 design describes it from Unreal 4.26 and adapted 5.x copies: the wish, the capsule's sizes, the base kept or not, ceilings, the in-air un-crouch, `K2_ComputeFloorDist`, the lift's sweep, the class default); a test that changes either compares the old tests' games frame by frame. `fake.useConfig` gives `main.lua` its settings as a table, or as config.lua's text, which Lua then reads as it would the file. `fake.loadMod` compiles `main.lua` and its modules once in a run and runs them again for each game, so every game gets fresh modules; a file changed on disk during a run isn't read again. `t.near` fails a value that isn't a number, and the tests' running maximums of the mod's numbers go through `t.max`, which does too (`math.max(0, 0/0)` is 0). `tests/test_slopes.lua` has a step-by-step model of Half-Life's updates on a plane (`halfLifeHops`), which the landings and slides are compared with, and a test that holds every number of the slope boosting table in `docs/TESTING.md` (on paper against the formulas, and every row in the fake game at 80 fps) and the sentences of the README, `config.lua` and `docs/TESTING.md` that quote its "every hop" speeds, word for word: change one, and the others have to follow (what it holds and doesn't: `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, "Tests"). Ducking's tests: `test_duck.lua` (`duck.lua` against the specs' rules), `test_physics.lua` (the duck per update, the 1 u/s stop), `test_hull.lua`, `test_eye.lua`, `test_keys.lua`, `test_duck_mod.lua` (through `main.lua`) and `test_duck_report.lua` (the report's ducking and keys lines).
- `docs/TESTING.md`: checklist for testing in the game, the ducking and keys steps D1 to D13, the console check, the slope boosting speeds and what each `Slope:` line of the log means, what to send, Remote Desktop.
- `docs/HISTORY.md`: what each version changed, and what earlier versions did that a rule here prevents. `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`: the figures measured in the fake game and against Half-Life that the rules here rest on, by this file's sections, each with what it was measured on. Every session and every helper reads this file in full, and those two only when it opens them. So a rule, its reason and the verdict on anything that was tried belong here; what a version fixed goes to the history (one paragraph under "Status"), and the figures behind a rule to the measurements. Before changing the code a section is about, or a number the docs quote, read that section of `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, and keep its figures true.

`lua5.4 tests/run.lua` and `luacheck .` must both pass (Ubuntu packages `lua5.4` and `lua-check`). The GitHub Actions workflow runs both and uploads the mod folder as an artifact. Tests also read files that aren't code. Every test game without settings of its own runs with the shipped `config.lua`, so it must load without a warning and keep every setting at its default, lists included: a changed value fails from a few tests to dozens, the first of them `test_settings`' checks of the file, which name the setting (for a list, they say only that the file differs). No test is known to depend on the frame phase: all 643 (0.4.1) pass with the 190 frames before each test made 191, 192, 193, 195, 200 or 215, and (measured on the 0.4.0 candidate) the 615 tests there were before `test_duck_report.lua`'s were added with anything from 187 to 215, or 225, 250 or 300 (at 185 and 186 the older "a mesh the game keeps invisible isn't started, ..." fails); a test that counts what the mod does over some frames waits for the automatic report first and checks that it came, and a test that reads a report reads only its own lines.

## Decisions so far

- **Lua only.** A UE4SS C++ mod needs the private UEPseudo repository (a GitHub account linked to Epic) and must be rebuilt for each UE4SS build.
- **UE4SS experimental builds.** The game is UE 5.4.4, which the stable 3.0.1 doesn't support. Point players to experimental-latest; builds from 1176 avoid the soft-pointer crash (issue 1433). Since 12 November 2026 there is a stable 4.0.0, and experimental-latest holds 4.0.0-1 (by the 0.3.1 review's reading of UE4SS's build workflow, build 1218's UE4SS source with a newer private Unreal submodule); neither has been tried with the mod. Both game tests used build 1218 (`UE4SS_v3.0.1-1218-g560411f1.zip`, still in UE4SS's release archive `experimental2`). The README says so; pointing players at 4.0 instead is the owner's call, best after a test on it. Keep the code working on build 848 and later:
  - compare objects with `GetAddress()`, never `==`;
  - copy structs through tables, never struct to struct;
  - don't rely on `LoopInGameThread*` or EngineTick: `EngineTickAvailable` may be false, and then `ExecuteInGameThread` throws;
  - UE4SS's own methods (`IsValid`, `GetWorld`, `GetFullName`, `IsA` and so on) shadow UFunctions of the same name;
  - always call game functions with `:` and every parameter; an out parameter takes a table, which UE4SS fills under the parameter's reflected name (`out.MainCamera` for `GetMainCamera`; SDK dumps show `MainCamera_0` because Dumper-7 renames parameters that share a member's name, so check a dump's `CallFunc_<Function>_<Param>` locals for the real name); a struct out parameter such as `FHitResult` is filled field by field, fields without a handler are skipped;
  - reading a field a struct doesn't have raises an error (read struct fields one at a time with `ue.get`/`ue.number`);
  - reading a name an object doesn't have (no property and no function) raises no error and never gives false: it gives an invalid object (to build 1005 a `UObject` wrapper around nullptr, whose call raises "Tried calling a member function but the UObject instance is nullptr"; from build 1008 a plain wrapper around a special invalid pointer, whose call raises "attempt to call a userdata value"; nil in build 1003; read in UE4SS's source, not tried in the game). So test a flag for the value it must have (`~= true`), not for its opposite (`== false`);
  - a `uint8` property (ByteProperty, such as ALS's `bRequestMovementSettingsChange`) is written with `lua_tointeger`, so a Lua `true` stores 0: write numbers;
  - `GetClass` on a null object crashes the game instead of raising an error, so `ue.className` and `ue.fullName` check `IsValid` first;
  - `ModRef:SetSharedVariable`/`GetSharedVariable` survive a mods restart and take nil, booleans, numbers, strings and objects. `ModRef.OnUnload` only exists from build 1181 and runs off the game thread, so it isn't used.
- **Hooks.** A post-hook on `BP_jRPG_Controller_World_C:ReceiveTick` drives every frame: it runs after input and before the character's movement tick. The fallback is the character's `ReceiveTick`, which runs after the movement component (`bTickBeforeOwner`), too late to take the input away: with it only first person (with its sounds) and the speedometer run, and the log says so. Since 0.4.1 a second, light post-hook on the character's `ReceiveTick` (`ViewTickFunctions`, registered by `ensureHooks` after the frame hook and never on the frame hook's own function, so not with the fallback) runs after Unreal's movement and before the view is worked out: Half-Life's view smoothing (see "Camera"). It fires for every character of the class: it returns at once unless the address is the mod's character. If it can't be registered, one line, and rises show at once as in 0.4.0; a hook that never comes for the mod's character (30 frames, paused ones not counted) gets one line too, and so does one that comes before Unreal's movement (30 frames on which it found the character where the frame hook had and Unreal moved it after; frames that didn't move, against a wall, tell nothing). The character doesn't tick while the game is paused (the pause frame only ticks the controller), so with the fallback the mod doesn't run then: the speedometer stays as it was, and the hotkeys' actions wait until the game runs again; the warning says both. Blueprint classes only load with a save, so hooks are added at load, at each `PlayerController:ClientRestart`, and from key callbacks while the frame hook is missing (F7 always tries the jump hook too), never from inside a hook: UE4SS's `script_hook` iterates its hook list by reference while it runs a callback, and `RegisterHook` appends to that list (use after free). Key callbacks may register hooks because UE4SS holds its recursive Lua lock (`m_thread_actions_mutex`) for them and for every script hook; the review of 0.2 refuted that this is a crash risk (UE4SS does its own object lookups, and moving them to `ExecuteInGameThread` could run `RegisterHook` inside `script_hook` with the ProcessEvent method). Before `RegisterHook`, the function is looked up with `StaticFindObject`, because a failed `RegisterHook` leaves a Lua thread behind. UE4SS runs the native `ClientRestart` callback without its lock, so that callback only adds hooks and sets a flag; the old character is put back on the next frame.
- **Hotkeys only queue their action;** the frame hook runs the queue. Each is bound plain and with Ctrl, Shift and both (`keys.bindAll`, most held first, each in its own `pcall`; UE4SS fires a bind only for exactly its modifiers; never Alt), since the duck keys are Ctrl and Shift; without UE4SS's `ModifierKey` table only plain, with one warning. One press runs its action once: an action already waiting isn't queued again, and a second callback with no frame in between and within 0.05 s is dropped (F7 then reads `config.lua` once); the callback notes the modifier held, which F8's report title names. Keybinds run on UE4SS's input thread, and acting on a key during a level load crashed another E33 mod. The key callback itself only does what doesn't touch the game: adding missing hooks, and for F7 reading `config.lua`. Before the frame hook exists, the action goes to `ExecuteInGameThread`; with no frame hook, F5 hooks in and turns the mod on. What the keys try again: switching the mod on with F5, first person on with F6, and F7 let first person start again after it was given up, the engine's first-person view too where it had failed; F5 and F7 turn on again every step that its errors turned off (`guard.reset`); only F5 turns the whole mod on again after it turned itself off; switching the speedometer on with F9, F5 and F7 try again a speedometer that couldn't be put on the screen. Nothing tries slopes again once the floor's normal couldn't be read (nor the movement curve once it couldn't be taken out, nor the world's clock once it didn't follow the pauses) until the game or UE4SS's mods restart. F7 also logs what the reload changed (`Settings reloaded ... Changed: PhysicsRate = 144 (was 100)`, or `Changed: nothing`; `settings.changes`), and starts every one of the log's line counts again. F8 only asks for the report, written at the end of a frame, not during the area-change wait or the 3 s settle: with the mod switched off or turned off by its errors, and with a character the mod leaves alone, it still waits for an area change to end and for a new character's first 3 s (a settle under way when the mod is switched off goes on), though not for 3 s after an area change that kept the character; at most 10 s after the first press, and pressing again doesn't start the wait again. The mod's clock goes on after it turned itself off, so these waits end. Before a save is loaded it reads nothing of the game: no `FindFirstOf`, which searches every object while the game may be loading. After the mod turned itself off, the report uses the controller of the frame it is written in.
- **Errors.** `guard.lua` runs each step on its own: the camera, the movement, turning the character, hiding the body, the pitch limits, the view check, the speed limiter, the speedometer, and since 0.4.0 placing the view at the eyes, taking the mod's keys from the game, watching the keys the mod gave back, ducking the character, the duck tap's lift and the floor below for standing up in the air, and since 0.4.1 smoothing the view after the movement (off by its errors: the eye as the frame hook writes it until F5 or F7). A step that fails 30 times in a row is turned off with its clean-up, without the others. Each different error is logged once (at most 20 lines until F5 or F7). The whole-frame breaker (30 failed frames turn the mod off) stays for the rest.
- **Half-Life first.** The owner's rule (2026-11-11): the movement should be as close to 1:1 with real Half-Life 1 as it can be. Where there is a choice in the movement, what Half-Life does wins and stays the default; a setting may offer something else (`AirSpeedCap`, `SlopeLandings = false`). Auto hop, on by default since 0.1, is Bunnymod XT's `bxt_autojump`, which the owner's Half-Life log was recorded with; since 0.4.0 it is decided per update with Half-Life's "jump is down" mark. +ducktap and its rule for a duck key held under it are OpenAG's (the owner plays OpenAG of May 2022: its 2022 rule, `duck.HELD_DUCK_UNDER_TAP = true`). Where OpenAG's `cl_autojump` would differ from Bunnymod XT's (+ducktap let go on the ground with jump held: OpenAG waits one or two updates; a held jump at a slide's end), the mod keeps Bunnymod XT's, the owner's to change after the test.
- **Movement.** Each frame the mod:
  - consumes the character's movement input (`ConsumeMovementInputVector`), so Unreal doesn't accelerate on its own; `GetCurrentAcceleration` on the next frame tells if game input still reached the movement component (logged, not fixed), checked once the mod moved the character on the two frames before (after a pause or a frame the mod left alone, the frame before may have been one Unreal ran as paused although `IsGamePaused` said otherwise);
  - works out the Half-Life velocity from the current `Velocity` and writes it back, in fixed steps of `1/PhysicsRate` s (default 250 per second since 0.4.0, Half-Life at fps_max 250, the rate duck tapping is played at, the owner's choice; 100 before; `physics.simulate`): leftover time carries to the next frame, each step gets the view of its own time (between the last frame's view and this frame's, the short way round, as each of Half-Life's updates has its own frame's view; a frame's turn shared out evenly among its steps reached them unevenly and lost strafing gain), at most 25 steps a frame, the jump decided per step (see "Ducking": a held key with `AutoHop` jumps on every step that is on the ground after its duck step, a notch only on the frame's first; a frame too short for a step jumps at once if its first step would, and not if that step lifts or stands up from the full duck; a jump notch there in the air waits for the next step). One step per frame loses air strafing speed against 250 below 250 fps (−22 % at 60 fps, −15 % at 80). `PhysicsRate` stops at 250: 25 steps cover a frame of 0.1 s, the longest the mod counts;
  - jumps with `SetMovementMode(3, 0)` plus Z velocity, and adds only the moving platform's velocity, read before the mode change with `GetImpartedMovementBaseVelocity` (falling back to the change `SetMovementMode` made); anything else the mode change did to the velocity is logged and ignored, since the speed limiter listens to mode changes. With the movement component's `bStayBasedInAir` (engine default false; false in E33), Unreal keeps carrying the character with its base and adds nothing, so the mod adds nothing either;
  - takes the platform's velocity (`GetImpartedMovementBaseVelocity`, read on the first frame on the ground) out of the velocity at a landing, as leaving the platform put it in (not with `bStayBasedInAir`): Unreal carries a based character with its base and leaves the velocity as it was in the air, so otherwise each hop on a moving platform adds its speed again. The hop lines give the speeds over the ground (with `bStayBasedInAir`, relative to the platform);
  - cancels braking: `bUseSeparateBrakingFriction = true` and `BrakingFriction = 0`, which also beats ALS's raised `BrakingFrictionFactor` after landing; `BrakingDecelerationWalking/Falling = 1`, not 0, because ALS's anim instance divides by `GetMaxBrakingDeceleration()`; takes out ALS's `CurrentMovementSettings.MovementCurve` every frame (it drives ground acceleration, braking and friction, and its braking would cost speed on each landing frame); and adds back `GetMaxBrakingDeceleration() * dt` on the velocity Unreal moves with, which after the mod's jump has the platform's velocity in it (on the velocity relative to the platform, hopping in place on a moving platform falls behind);
  - sets `MaxWalkSpeed` and `MaxWalkSpeedCrouched` huge every frame, because ALS and the game rewrite them (ALS both at each stance change; each rewrite is logged, the first 3 until F7), and keeps the game's values for that frame's cut check; checks once that `GetMaxSpeed()` follows it;
  - on every ground update that doesn't jump, with a wish or without, sets a horizontal speed under 1 u/s after friction and the ground acceleration to 0 (Half-Life's; two of the owner's demo's updates need it, with no movement key; 0.4.0 for all walking);
  - leaves the ground three ways, each with `SetMovementMode(3, 0)` and only the platform's velocity kept: its jump, a launch off a slope ("Slopes"), and the duck tap's lift ("Ducking");
  - pauses the speed limiter `BP_VelocityControllerComponent_C` (the character's `BP_VelocityControllerComponent` property, or found among its components), checked every frame: it clamps to `GetMaxSpeed()` and is wired to `MovementModeChangedDelegate`, so it can come back on at every jump and landing;
  - with `KeepSpeed` (default), undoes a speed cut: the horizontal speed read back at most 97 % of what the mod wrote last frame, with the direction within 2°, although the character moved at least 90 % (and at most twice) of the way that velocity would take it in that frame, and the speed read back is under 97 % of the speed it really moved at (`K2_GetActorLocation` before and after, minus the velocity of the moving platform it stood on when the mod wrote, read then; a platform that reports no velocity still counts). Walking off a ledge, the speed expected is what the mod wrote plus that platform's velocity, which Unreal adds then (not with `bStayBasedInAir`). A frame over `MaxSimulationTimeStep` (0.05 s by default) is walked in several moves, which can take the character from a moving platform onto still ground and off its ledge with nothing added, so a speed that comes back as the mod wrote it isn't a cut either, and a cap at the mode change that leaves between 97 % of the mod's speed and 97 % of that plus the platform's isn't undone. A wall hit head on or a failed step-up shortens the move and `PhysWalking` sets the velocity from the distance moved, so neither counts. The distance isn't checked when the movement mode changed during Unreal's update (a landing, walking off a ledge), since the limiter's handler may cap the speed inside the move; a wall hit in such a frame counts as a cut for that frame, and so would a game launch that keeps less of the speed (none is known in E33). Below 20 % it only counts if the limiter had just been started again or the speed is `GetMaxSpeed()` or a `MaxWalkSpeed` the game wrote since the last frame (within 1 %, 3D or horizontal); otherwise it is the game stopping the character. Cuts are logged (the first 5 until F7, with GetMaxSpeed, MaxWalkSpeed and the limiter's state, "not paused" when `DisableSpeedLimiter` is false);
  - writes the character's `bBreakfallOnLand = false` every frame it moves the character, first and third person alike (0.4.1, `LandingRoll`, default false: Half-Life has no roll): the owner's first test of 0.4.0 saw ALS's breakfall roll at landings with a movement key held (E33's `BreakfallOnLandVelocity` is 0, so from any fall), whose root motion hands the movement to the game and loses the speed. Through the movement's overrides: one read a frame, the game's later value is what comes back at the release, journaled; only on a character where the flag reads as a boolean. `LandingRoll = true` leaves it;
  - logs a sudden loss (0.4.1, `speedLoss`): on a frame after one the mod moved the character, the horizontal speed Unreal moved with (after KeepSpeed's undo and ALS's braking given back, before a landing's platform correction) under 75 % of what the mod wrote and lower by at least 100 u/s, where `checkCut` didn't take it for a cut: one line (`Speed lost: from X to Y u/s in one frame (walking or in the air, standing/ducking/ducked, heading, the move got N % of its way; at X Y Z): the character most likely ran into something`, or `: the game set the speed itself (nothing cut the move short)` when the move got 90 % of its way or more with the mode unchanged; walking off a ledge the speed expected is the lower of what the mod wrote and that plus the platform's velocity, as in `checkCut`; 10 until F7), counted for the status line and the report, and the hop's line ends `, ran into something, -N u/s` (or `, the game set the speed, -N u/s`). No game call: the location is the one the movement reads anyway. Made for the owner's hop 22 in that test (699 to 232 u/s, ducked, every cut undone), which nothing logged;
  - sets gravity through `GravityScale`, and sets `JumpZVelocity` and `JumpMaxHoldTime` so the game's own first jump matches the mod's;
  - logs each hop (fps, updates per frame, take-off and landing speed, air time, view turn, keys, cuts; a frame over 0.1 s counts in full in the air time, the fps and the turn rate, and in a flight's landing line, as Unreal moves all of it) and a status line for every 10 s in which something happened: a hop, the character moving (a speed of 1 cm/s or more written), the view turning 1° or more (yaw), or the game cutting the speed, starting its limiter, setting `MaxWalkSpeed` or steering. Ten seconds paused or standing still get no line and use none up.
- **The log.** `log.lua` writes plain lines, warnings, lines said once (`log.once`), a line before the first use of each kind of change to the game (`log.first`, so that after a crash the log's last line shows how far the mod got) and state lines with reasons (`log.state`); the movement adds its hop, slope and status lines, and `diagnostics.lua` the report. It has to stay short in normal play and still hold every part of a test.
  - What a hop is: a jump of the mod's that is still in the air on the next frame. Where the floor rises faster than the jump, Unreal lands the jump again inside the frame it started in: on a walkable slope taken fast enough that in the jump's first frame the floor makes up the jump's own rise (268 u/s less half a frame of gravity, since Unreal moves with the average of the old and the new speed) and the 2.15 cm Unreal keeps under a walking capsule, from (268 − 400/fps + 0.86 × fps) / tan θ u/s (at 80 fps 575 on 30° and 396 on 40°; in steps of 5 u/s the fake game's are 580 and 400). A landing like that starts a slide (`SlopeBoosts`, on a floor the mod can use); without one (both slope settings off, or a floor that can't be read) AutoHop with the key held jumps again on every frame and the speed stays, and with `SlopeBoosts = false` alone it is the odd jump only. Such a jump isn't a hop (no hop number or line, not in the per-jump numbers); it is counted (`groundedJumps`) in the status line ("6 hops (and 125 jumps that landed again within their own frame)") and the report, and one log line marks the first. Just below that speed the jump lands one to three frames later: those are hops ("0.01 s in the air"). The movement there is left as it was (two movement mode changes and a `Landed` on every frame; what E33 does with them is unknown).
  - Line counts. The log takes so many lines of each kind and then waits for F7: 30 hop lines, 30 tap lines, 30 "Duck:" lines (ducks and stand-ups), 30 status lines, 30 "Slope:" lines for landings that ran into a slope, 30 for landings on a slope that kept their speed and 30 for slides and flights, 5 for the game's speed cuts, 10 "Speed lost:" lines and 10 "Root motion:" lines (0.4.1), and 3 for each other thing the game did (its limiter started again, `MaxWalkSpeed` set, the velocity changed at a jump, ALS's braking at a crouch or stand-up, no room to stand up, a pop-up, the game's own crouch, ALS clearing the crouch wish, the game's gait put back), each state text 10 times and 200 state lines in all, 20 error lines. F7 starts every one of these counts again (`movement.logAgain`, `log.again`, `mod.statusLines`, `guard.reset`); F5, F6 and F8 don't, except that F5 turning the mod on also starts the error lines again. The counts behind the status lines and the report go on for the session (`st.counts`; the lines about what the game did have their own, `st.noteLines`), and a hop line keeps the session's hop number. Not counts: the once-only lines and the 3 lines per start of first person about the game re-attaching its camera. Two kinds of line count for the session, 3 each: hidden parts the game moved to another actor (`camera.lua`) and a speedometer the game took off the screen (`speedometer.lua`); both numbers also feed the report (`hidden parts the game moved to another actor this session=N` in its `First person:` line, `taken off the screen by the game N times` in its `Speedometer:` line), so starting the lines again would need a second counter in each. `docs/TESTING.md` says where to press F7 (standing on the ground: in mid-hop F7 loses that hop's line, and it ends a slide or a flight), so that each part fits in 30; a leap down a slope takes 2 slide and flight lines and a landing line. The landings need a count for each kind as large as the hop lines', because all the landings of a hill can be one kind and a hop line gives neither the slope's angle nor the fall. Without F7 five minutes of strafing hops (or +ducktap taps) leave 244 lines, 163 of them the automatic report.
- **Facing** (`BodyFollowsHop`, default true; `false` gives 0.2.6's behaviour). Figures: `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, "Facing".
  - Why: in the air ALS turns the body towards its `InAirRotation` (`RInterpTo` at 5 per second), and sets that to the body's own rotation whenever the character leaves the ground; only the game's own jump (`OnJumped`, ALS's `EventOnJumped`) sets the velocity's direction instead, above 100 cm/s. The mod's jump (`SetMovementMode(3, 0)`) never calls `OnJumped`, and with AutoHop a landing frame is the only one on which ALS turns the body on the ground, so in 0.2.6 the body kept its take-off facing and hopped sideways or backwards.
  - On each frame the mod moves the character in the air, after its jump (which makes ALS reset the value), it writes `InAirRotation = (0, the led heading, 0)` and `TargetRotation = (0, the heading, 0)`: ALS's in-air update copies `InAirRotation` into `TargetRotation`, but a landing frame's one grounded update starts from `TargetRotation`, and with the led value there the body jumped 7-16° ahead at each landing.
  - The heading is the yaw of the horizontal velocity the mod hands to Unreal, apart from the moving platform's velocity that leaving it added (the platform velocity the mod's jump kept, none with `bStayBasedInAir`; at a walk-off, the base velocity read when the mod last wrote; none after a frame the mod didn't move the character), so hopping in place on a moving platform doesn't turn the body to the platform's way. Below 100 cm/s (ALS's threshold) nothing is written, except on the first such frame right after one with a heading: it writes that last heading, not led, into both values, once (AutoHop's jump on a landing frame counts as such a frame; its write comes after ALS's reset), or a stop in mid-air while turning (a wall hit head on) leaves the body turned up to 45° past the heading, on the ground too.
  - The lead makes up for ALS's 5/s smoothing: the heading's turn rate × (1/5 − the frame's length), at most 45° either way. The rate is the heading's turn over the time the frame's Half-Life updates stand for (their number ÷ `PhysicsRate`; with `PhysicsRate` 0 the time since the last frame that ran one), and a frame that runs none keeps it (a rate from one frame alone made the target jump about 45° and back at 144 fps). It starts again whenever the run of air frames breaks: a frame below 100 cm/s or one the mod didn't move the character in (AutoHop's jump on the frame after a landing continues the run). As each update turns the heading as the view turned in it, the rate reads the view's turn rate at any frame rate.
  - The rate isn't averaged. One update turns the velocity by at most about `AirAccelerate` × `MaxSpeed` ÷ `PhysicsRate` ÷ the speed (in radians; 733° ÷ the speed in u/s with the defaults, 1830° at `PhysicsRate` 100); while that is as far as the view turns in an update (at 180°/s up to about 1020 u/s), the heading follows the view update by update, a frame with two updates turns it and the target twice as far as one with one, and ALS turns the body evenly, as the view turns. A mouse that reports 125 times a second makes the target jump at any speed, and the body still turns within about 1° of an even turn. Averaging the rate (tried in the 0.3 review) kept the body steadier but further from the heading after each change between A and D, so it isn't done; an average that takes any change over 150°/s at once cost almost nothing at those changes: the one to try if a shimmer is ever seen in third person. If the game's in-air rate isn't 5, the body is ahead of the heading by about w × (1/5 − 1/r).
  - First person with `BodyFollowsView` stays as in 0.2.6: `camera.faceView` turns the body to the view on the ground, in the air and in the jump frame. While the mod moves the character in the air (its jump included), `InAirRotation` is set to the view's yaw too, so ALS doesn't pull the hidden body back towards its take-off facing.
  - One guarded step, "turning the character to where it heads": 30 failures in a row turn it off, and the movement goes on. Nothing is put back or journaled: only ALS's in-air update reads `InAirRotation`, and ALS sets it again at every take-off. After a release in mid-air (battle, area change, F5), or from a frame in mid-air on which the mod leaves the movement alone (root motion, locked input), it holds the led target, up to 45° past the direction of travel, until the mod writes it again or the next take-off, which is harmless: the game moves the character itself then. The one write of the last heading isn't made up when the frame right after the stop is one the mod doesn't move the character in, a pause included: the body then stays turned.
  - The hop lines end with ", body ±N deg from where it headed" (+: to the right): the body's yaw (`K2_GetActorRotation`, one call per logged hop) at the top of the hop (the first air frame where the vertical speed the mod writes is 0 or less), against the heading, not led, of the frame before; only for hops the heading step steered, so not in first person. Reading N: it tells about ALS only in hops that turned slower than about 240°/s (the line's own deg/s), where it stays within about 3 of 0 in the fake game; with an in-air rate r instead of 5 a hop that turns one way at w °/s reads about −w × (1/r − 1/5) on a turn to the right (r = 4 reads −9 at 180°/s, r = 3 −24, r = 8 +13; less in hops that switch direction). Faster turns trail by design: the lead stops at 45° from 45 ÷ (1/5 − the frame's length) °/s on (240°/s at 80 fps), so N reads 9 to 15 behind at 300°/s and 18 to 27 at 360°/s holding one strafe key, less switching A and D. The hop during which F6 switched to third person reads large too (the body starts where first person had it). N about as large as a hop's whole turn, in slow hops too, means E33 doesn't use `InAirRotation` as ALS-Community does. The report shows ALS's `ViewMode`, `Speed`, `TargetRotation`, `InAirRotation`, `LastVelocityRotation` and `AimYawRate`, the body's rotation, `CurrentMovementSettings.RotationRateCurve` at 1, 2 and 3, and the engine's `bOrientRotationToMovement`, `bUseControllerDesiredRotation` and `bUseControllerRotationYaw`.
- **Slopes** (`SlopeLandings` and `SlopeBoosts`, both default true; both false give 0.2.6's slopes). The figures, and how the mod was checked against Half-Life: `docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, "Slopes".
  - Why: Unreal keeps the horizontal speed on any walkable slope, walking or landing, never lifts a walking character, and in the air never lets a slide rise (`HandleSlopeBoosting`). Half-Life clips the velocity against what it runs into (overbounce 1) and counts a player rising faster than 180 u/s as in the air: a landing that runs into a downslope gains speed, one that runs into an upslope loses it, and hitting an upward slope fast lifts you into a slide without friction that flies off the top (slope boosting). But Half-Life only clips a landing whose move reaches the floor inside one of its updates: its ground check, once per update, puts a player within 2 units above a walkable floor on it with the velocity untouched (the layer that makes jumpbugs and edgebugs), so most landings of a plain hop keep their speed. And its ground move goes straight ahead: running down a slope, where the floor falls away under one update's move further than the ground check reaches, its player is in the air after that update and leaps down the slope without friction, where Unreal walks a character down any walkable slope.
  - No new traces. The floor's normal is the movement component's `CurrentFloor.HitResult.ImpactNormal`, read field by field, only on landing frames, during a slide, above 360 u/s (slower can't slide: sin θ cos θ ≤ 0.5), and where the character walked down so steeply in the frame before that an update could leave the floor (a descent of at least 1.5 units an update, `DESCENT_SHARE` 0.75 of the 2 units, from the locations read every frame anyway), so walking on flat ground below 360 u/s costs nothing; a slide also reads `FloorDist` on each frame. Under 1° is flat, which covers a step's edge with a flat tread. A sloped floor whose contact normal (`HitResult.Normal`) is more than 8° off the surface's isn't used: the capsule rests on an edge or a crease of it (the lip of a ramp, a ridge). A line-trace floor isn't used either. 30 unreadable reads in a row turn slopes off for the session, with one log line; only a floor that reads starts the count again, not the movement being given back and taken over.
  - The landing rule (`SlopeLandings`). Half-Life looks for the ground once per update: a player not rising faster than 180 u/s with a walkable floor's plane less than 2 units below is put on it with its velocity untouched, and a held jump starts at once; only if no update ends that close does the next update's move run into the floor, and only then is the velocity clipped. So a landing changes the speed in full or not at all, decided by where the last update before the floor ends, which is a matter of millimetres (kz-rush's "EdgeBug & JumpBug physics", jwchong.com/hl and Quake's source agree, and the owner's Half-Life log shows it; no SDK source was read). Clipping every landing, as 0.3.0 did, makes a crawl of any hill.
  - How the mod decides a landing (`landingKind`, whose comment has the derivation): it works out where the character was at the last update before it came down, from its location at the hook before (read every frame for KeepSpeed), the velocity written there, that frame's length, its location now and the floor's normal. Unreal's fall is a parabola and it walks on along the floor after touching down, so the plane the character stands on goes through the landing spot, and the height above it, straight down as the ground check measures, was h0 + a t − g t²/2 with h0 = ((before − now) · n) / n.z and a = v.z + (v.x n.x + v.y n.y) / n.z (a moving platform's travel and velocity taken out, its Z too; with `bStayBasedInAir`, the travel of the platform that carried the character in the air, Z too). Half-Life's player rests 1/32 unit off the plane, at right angles to it: its moves stop that far short and the ground check moves it down to there, never up, so a player set down from lower stays lower for its next hop (`st.landRest`, `st.takeoffRest`). Unreal holds a walking capsule 1.9 to 2.4 cm above the floor (`CurrentFloor.FloorDist`, 2.15 after a landing): the mod reads it where its jump or launch starts and at a landing on a slope (`floorGap`, `st.takeoffGap`) and takes the difference out (without both readings, or one outside 0 to 3 cm, they are taken as equal; half a centimetre is what a hop on the spot has to spare). The updates are counted from the hop's take-off, 1/`PhysicsRate` s apart (`st.airNext`; with `PhysicsRate` 0 the hooks are the updates): in Half-Life a hop starts on an update, so each hop gets Half-Life's own ground checks at any frame rate (counted from the simulate's accumulator, as first tried, the mod matched a step-by-step reference only at exactly `PhysicsRate` frames a second). The game's own jump counts from the hook before the one that first sees it in the air; a walk-off from the hook that first sees it, as Half-Life's fall starts at the end of the update that walked off, whose second half of gravity stays on the vertical speed: −g/(2 · `PhysicsRate`), −4 u/s at 100, which the mod takes off there (with `PhysicsRate` 0, half of the last frame's gravity; not for the game's own jump or the end of a slide). In the game the capsule probably rounds the ledge's edge and starts falling lower (not checked): the mod gives Half-Life's fall from where Unreal starts it, not Half-Life's landing for a given ledge. A pause in mid-hop doesn't count. If the last update that began before the player came down to its plane ended less than 2 units above it, not rising faster than 180 u/s, the landing is set down: nothing is clipped and no slide starts (where several updates end within reach, the first that did is the one the log names). Otherwise it ran into the slope: the velocity (horizontally after the KeepSpeed check, relative to a platform; vertically the update that hit, half an update on) is clipped against the floor and the clipped horizontal velocity used. Unreal lands a moment after Half-Life's player came down, and the parabola is followed back; a frame over `MaxSimulationTimeStep` is decided like any other (Unreal's steps follow the same parabola). No game call is added: at a landing on a slope `FloorDist` is one more field of the `CurrentFloor` read there, at the take-off the same if the floor was read in that frame (else `CurrentFloor` and three of its fields), and nothing is read in the air.
  - A flight with a hull of its own (0.4.0): a duck tap's lift, a take-off fully ducked, a landing ducked. Half-Life's ducked hull rests lower on a floor: on Valve's maps the compiler expands each brush for each hull, so the ducked floor lies `duck.restGap(nz)` units below the standing one, 18 nz rounded to whole units (17 on the owner's 17.7° ramp, which his log shows; the exact 18 there would catch his ducked hop on the spot with 0 u/s where Half-Life clipped it to 90.47). So a take-off fully ducked starts from that floor (a slow one reads `CurrentFloor` once for the slope), a landing ducked ends on it, and the lift adds `st.takeoffLift` (Half-Life's player 18 units up where the capsule was placed 16 1/32 less the walking gap up, about 2.8 units higher all the way down). With those terms Half-Life's player is still above its plane at Unreal's contact, so the rule checks the character's own path against the plane (a landing on another floor is still told apart) and follows Half-Life's past the frame's end on the frame's path by their positive part (`ahead`; with `PhysicsRate` 0 the update past the frame is the one this hook begins); without it every tap landing on a slope was "can't tell". For a flight without a hull of its own `ahead` is 0 and nothing changes. The look-back stops at the last hull change in the air (`w.hullAir`). Against a model of Half-Life's updates, 95 of 95 tap landings come out the same kind, and the owner's ducked hop on the spot lands at 90 u/s at `PhysicsRate` 100 as his log has it (`docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, "At PhysicsRate 250").
  - What the mod can't tell, and does then: the speed stays, as Unreal landed it, and the landing is counted and logged as not told apart. That is when there is nothing from the frame before (a pause), after a frame over 0.1 s, when the game's gravity couldn't be read, and when the floor isn't the plane the character came down on (more than 3 cm under it while still in the air, or a path that doesn't reach it within the frame: a step, a ledge, a crest). Still rising faster than 180 u/s after the whole frame's gravity, it was in the air for Half-Life on every update, so it counts as run into (see the landing in mid-rise). Known to differ from Half-Life, and left: a flight the mod didn't start has no `FloorDist` from its take-off; where Half-Life's player rests is kept only from a landing on a slope to the next take-off (else 1/32 unit off the plane it lands on, at right angles to it: 1/32 × (1/cos θ − 1) higher than Half-Life's after a walk-off from a level ledge, 0.005 units on 30°); a landing just past a bend where the slope gets steeper is decided on the steeper plane extended back under the last update, below the real floor there, so it can be clipped where Half-Life sets it down (the floor under that update would need a trace); with `PhysicsRate` 0 the first update within reach isn't looked for (the lengths of the frames before the last two aren't kept): the kind and the speed are the same, but the log names the last update before the floor, up to nearly 2 units lower where more than one update ends within reach, and the next hop's resting height can be up to 1/32 unit low. If UE4SS can't read `FloorDist` in the game, the two distances are taken as equal.
  - What hills do. At the default 250 a plain hop closes in on an even slope at under 2 units an update unless the speed along the fall line times tan θ is over about 232 u/s going down (on 10° from about 1310 u/s, on 20° from 640), so at running and strafing speeds no landing on an even hill or a gentle tilt runs into it, as in Half-Life at fps_max 250; the rest of this part acts at lower rates. A landing that runs into the hill changes the speed by the clip's share of the fall (from a run about −36 u/s going up 10° and +55 going down). Which landings run into it depends on millimetres, so the docs promise players no number "per hop" and no speed after so many seconds on a hill. A hop comes down on a slope as fast as it rose from it at the take-off, 268 u/s less the speed times the slope's tangent, so one that takes off faster up a slope than a tangent-speed of 68 u/s is always set down at 100 updates a second, and every plain hop along level ground or an even rise from 135 updates a second; where the ground turns upward under a hop (the foot of a hill, the bottom of a dip) it comes down faster than it rose, so such a landing can still run into it from 135 on, less often the higher the rate, as in Half-Life. With W and jump only on an even slope the hops repeat, and the speed changes at one or two landings at most and then stays; only strafing makes the hops differ. Hopping on the spot on a slope stays on the spot (67 updates a hop, 0.22 units above where it started, as in the log; at 250, 166 updates and 1.81 units) at `PhysicsRate` 100 and from 135 up; at some lower rates (80, 95, 98 and 101 of those tried) the first landings run into the slope and it moves on down it, as today's Steam Half-Life does at that frame rate (the 2005 version, of these, only at 80: see "An update's length"), so also with `PhysicsRate` 0 at 80 fps. Hops along a slope that rises under them by 68 to about 220 u/s (speed × tangent) are always set down and climb in short hops; above that they come back to the slope still rising faster than 180 u/s, run into it and slide, so a steep hill limits the hopping speed by itself.
  - Checked against Half-Life itself and against reference simulations of its updates (a point above a plane; `tests/test_slopes.lua` has a small copy of the loop): fed the owner's log's update lengths as frame times (`PhysicsRate` 0), the mod lands the 100 hops of the log it can do as the log has them, and hop by hop against an independent reference 17,856 of 17,856 landings come out the same. The references, the probes and the log stayed in the reviews' scratch folders.
  - Slide (`SlopeBoosts`): a landing that runs into the slope, or running into an upward slope, starts one if the rise the clip leaves is still over 180 u/s half an update of gravity later (over 181.6 u/s at 250 updates a second, 184 at 100: `staysUp`; Half-Life's ground check at the next update comes after the second half of that update's gravity). Running into an upward slope is decided per update in `physics.simulate`, after the update's ground move (Half-Life's order): `slopeStep` arms it (`dk.runIn`) where Unreal walks fast on a readable sloped floor and after a landing Half-Life leaves on the ground; the update that runs into the slope is a ground update (its duck step, friction, ground acceleration), the slide starts from the next one, and never on an update that jumps or lifts (0.3.1 decided at the hook, from the velocity before the update's friction, and started the slide on the first update: a +ducktap press on a ramp was lifted where Half-Life slides). The update that took the character onto the slope was the last of the frame before (Unreal's walk did it), so at the next walking hook what it left is clipped against the new floor first; a rise over 180 u/s there that doesn't stay up marks the next update's duck button as built in the air (`d.airSeen`: OpenAG's client builds it from the last ground check, which finds the player in the air in that band, 1.6 u/s of rise at 250). At a landing the slide wins (Half-Life finds the player in the air before it jumps). A set-down landing starts none: the held jump fires, and if the slope rises faster than the jump (speed × tangent over 264 u/s) the jump's own update runs into it and slides a frame later, with nearly all the speed; without a jump the run-in starts it on the update after the landing. Unreal keeps walking the character up the slope; the mod keeps the vertical speed, hands Unreal a horizontal velocity and runs the slide update by update (`physics.simulate`, `slideMove`): Half-Life's ground check, the update's gravity, the air acceleration (no friction, no jump), and a clip only if the move would end behind the floor's plane. The path is followed (`slide.h`, its height above the plane, straight up, from 1/32 unit off it after the clip that began the slide, or lower where the landing left it): a move that falls short isn't clipped, after a clip the player is 1/32 unit off the plane (or where it was, if closer), and a move away raises the path; so above about 160 updates a second a slide that starts within one and a half updates of gravity of 180 u/s is on the ground again an update or a few later, as in Half-Life. Each frame the floor under the path is measured again: the location less `CurrentFloor.FloorDist`, plus the plane's rise over the path's lead (`slide.ox`, `oy`: the updates' travel less the velocity Unreal walks the character with, times the frame; a time lead, tried first, made clips into misses on a slowing slide). Where the floor fell away under the path (a top that rounds off, a step Unreal walks down) the path is above it, and the clips the frame's updates made against the old plane after the path left the floor are taken back (as if the floor bent in one place). A frame over 0.1 s gets the gravity of the rest of it in one piece (`slideRest`), as Unreal walks all of it, starts the path again from the floor, and stops the slide if that would turn it back down. Where the path would rise more than 8 units (20 cm) above the floor, the mod launches it with `SetMovementMode(3, 0)` as at its jump (the platform's velocity read first, other changes ignored, the heading for `BodyFollowsHop` from then on); Half-Life's 2-unit layer made strings of tiny launches and landings on bumpy slopes. The decision uses the rise less a frame's gravity (0.1 s at most) and before the take-back. A flight off a slope (a launch, or Unreal walking off the end) starts with the rise the slide had, as Unreal's falling update takes the frame's gravity off itself (walking off the end, Unreal's walk follows the slope's line for the whole frame, so the rise is about half a frame's gravity high, while in the game the capsule may start it lower: kept until a test in the game says otherwise). The slide is over at the start of the update that finds the rise at 180 u/s or less and the floor within the ground check's 2 units of the path, half an update of gravity after the last move, with the horizontal velocity it left and no new clip; that update and the rest of the frame's are on the ground, and with `AutoHop` a held jump starts on it (without it, a press during the slide was used up in the air, as in Half-Life). Out of reach then, the character flies on with that velocity. A slide ends at the reference's speed within 0.01 u/s at `PhysicsRate` 100, 144, 200, 250 and 0. Since 0.4.0 an update's slide gravity comes after its duck step, and only while it still slides: a duck in a slide ends it in a launch with the rise the slide had (that update is free flight, whose gravity Unreal takes), and a stand-up in a slide is refused.
  - How fast (`docs/TESTING.md` has the table for the owner, held by a test). Running into a slope without the jump key starts a slide when the speed an update leaves after its friction, times sin θ cos θ, is over 180 u/s plus half an update of gravity (181.6 at 250, 184 at 100): from that ÷ (sin θ cos θ (1 − `Friction` / rate)), 1080, 739, 575, 482 and 427 u/s on 10° to 30° in the fake game. On the ground Half-Life's friction takes 4 u/s per unit run (160 u/s a metre), and with AutoHop a held jump fires first, so a run-in only happens with the jump key let go and a landing within about half a metre of the foot. A hop from level ground with the jump held: a landing that runs into the slope slides above (181.6 + fall × sin² θ) / (sin θ cos θ) (184 at 100), the fall counted as negative while it still rises; the others are set down, and the hop their held jump starts slides when it comes back to the slope still rising faster than 180 u/s, once the slope rises under it by about 220 u/s (speed × tangent). Every approach slides, at the landing or in the hop after it, from the table's "every hop" speed; on 20° to 30° the last to slide is the landing that falls at the full 268 u/s just past the foot (at 250 nearly every slide begins in the hop after a set-down landing or, on 25° and 30°, at the held jump's landing; on 10° and 15° nothing slides below the speed from which all do). A slide then loses g sin θ cos θ a second and ends with a rise between 181.6 u/s (184 at 100) and that less one update's loss. So on a slope that ends in a crest, only a short slope, a landing near its top or much more speed gives a flight off the top, which starts with the slide's rise; a top that rounds off also throws you where the floor falls more than 2 units away under the slide before its rise is down to 180 u/s. To the player a slide looks like running up the slope: Unreal walks the character, and with the jump key held the hops stop while it lasts. With `SlopeBoosts = false` the same landing goes on in quick low hops and keeps more speed uphill (each jump gets its rise for nothing, while a slide pays for its climb). The owner's best in the second test was 726 u/s, so `docs/TESTING.md` gives a shortcut to the table's speeds, `MaxSpeed = 800` with `InputSpeed = 800` (see "Units and defaults"): slides in a log may come from it (F7's `Changed:` line and the report's `Settings not at their defaults` say so), and with W held and no jump key a new slide begins each time one ends.
  - A landing in mid-rise that doesn't go into the floor: Unreal lands a character that still rises when its capsule catches the edge of a ledge (its falling update has no check of the vertical speed), which can happen in the first 0.1 s of a hop, and a flight can come down on a steep part of rough ground with the frame ending on a flatter one. The clip then changes nothing, but above 180 u/s Half-Life still has the player in the air, so the slide's rules apply: no jump, and on the next frame the character flies off, with the rise it had, if its path leaves the floor by more than 8 units. If not, Unreal walks it, and within 0.1 s either the rise is down to 180 u/s (the slide ends and a held jump fires; with the path still more than 2 units above the slope it flies on and lands first) or the path has come back to the slope and it slides up it. The log says "the game landed you on a N degree slope at X u/s while still rising Y u/s, away from it: Half-Life keeps you in the air above 180", and the hop line "(still in the air)". On a flat top it is a plain landing, and on a plain ramp it can't happen. Both other ways were tried in the 0.3 review: landing it there ended slides early on bumpy slopes, and launching it at once added a launch and a landing each time.
  - The 8 units: a slope with bumps of ±4° in 1 m segments is slid up without a launch at up to 800 u/s at every frame rate from 30 to 240 (7 units launched at 144 and 240 fps, 6 from 80 fps up); faster, a slide flies off bumps and lands again, because the launch goes by the rise after the frame's gravity (8 units stand for about 10 units of real rise at 60 fps, 8.5 at 240). If the launch is ever decided from the rise before the frame's gravity, 10 units keep what 8 do now at 60 fps. These hold at 250 too. Whether 8 suits E33's ground is for the owner's test to say (`docs/TESTING.md`, step 9).
  - Running down a slope (`SlopeBoosts`): where the floor falls 2 units, less the 1/32 Half-Life's player rests above it, under one update's straight move (speed × tan θ / `PhysicsRate`: at the default 250 from 1352 u/s on 20°, 852 on 30°, 587 on 40° and 510 on 44°, never on 10° (over `MaxVelocity`) and never at a run; at 100 from 1116, 734, 540, 422, 340, 280 and 233 u/s on 10° to 40°, 197 on Unreal's steepest walkable 44.77°, and 1.44 times those at 144; a plain run of 320 u/s leaves slopes over 31.5° at 100 and over 41.4° at 144), the player is in the air after that update, without friction, until it comes down on the slope again (a landing like any other), and leaves again at the end of one update on the ground, the only one with friction (1.6 % at 250, 4 % at 100). Unreal walks a character down any walkable slope, so the mod has it leave as at the top of a slide (`SetMovementMode(3, 0)`, no vertical speed, the platform's velocity read first): at a frame's start if the last update ran on the ground (`st.groundSteps`) and its velocity leaves this floor, whatever the jump key, since Half-Life's player is in the air then (with Space held throughout, every landing jumps before any update on the ground, so none is due); or at one of the frame's updates (`physics.simulate`'s `floor`, `physics.leavesFloor`), which leaves the rest of the frame's updates in the air (a first fix that launched only at a frame's start gave two updates of friction a leap below `PhysicsRate` frames a second). Not while the character's `bPressedJump` is set (read only then: Unreal does the game's jump). The floor is read for it on landing frames, above 360 u/s, and below that where the descent in the frame before reaches 1.5 units an update. The log has a line for each leap ("flew off a 20 degree slope going down it at 700 u/s (the floor falls 2.5 units in one Half-Life update, out of the ground check's reach of 2)"), counted with the launches. Half-Life's update that left moved straight ahead and ended in the air, c/`PhysicsRate` above the slope (c: how fast the floor falls under the velocity) and falling g/(2 · `PhysicsRate`); the mod's flight starts from the floor at 0 u/s. On an even slope the heights differ by (c − g t/2)/`PhysicsRate` t seconds into the flight, nothing where they come down (t = 2c/g), so the last update above the slope is as high as Half-Life's and the landing comes out the same kind; but Half-Life's player comes down g/(2 · `PhysicsRate`) faster (4 u/s at 100; with `PhysicsRate` 0, half of the leaving frame's): the leap keeps that (`leaveFall`) and its landing takes it off the fall it clips. - **Strafe gain.** The defaults are Half-Life's (`AirSpeedCap` 30, `PhysicsRate` 250 since 0.4.0). At 100 updates a second the best wish angle is 90°, gaining the cap squared in v² per update (tested); at 250 an update's acceleration (12.8 u/s) is under the cap, so the best angle is a little under 90° and the gain 604 in v² an update. A strafe bot on `physics.simulate` gains what Half-Life does at an fps_max of its `PhysicsRate` (100 in the test) at any frame rate, since each update gets the view of its own time (a test holds it to 0.01 u/s from 30 to 240 fps and with uneven frames), and matches the owner's 0.2.6 hops within about 10 % with two or three turns a hop: the gain is limited by how far the view turns (at most about 0.5 u/s per degree), and switching A/D 50 ms late halves it. Since 0.4.0 it also has the ducking block (the hull's way and why, Half-Life's duck and Unreal's crouch now, the counts since loading, ALS's braking, the floor queries, the re-bases in the air, the detectors), the crouch fields of the movement component and the character, the class default's (found by its class's path, with its `CharacterMovement`), the capsule's collision profile, ALS's crouching `MovementData`, the keys now and why, the frames the movement ran while the game showed a dialogue, a cinematic or another view target, the game's actions on the mod's keys at the last take and now, the legacy `ActionMappings` on the mod's keys, the wheel and `Zoom`, and the keys seen pressed and ignored; F8's title names a modifier held. Since 0.4.1: the `view smoothing` line (its hook, frames run and lagging, the largest lag, late writes, frames in a row without the hook), the sudden speed losses and root motions with their lines left, and `LandingRoll` with the game's own `bBreakfallOnLand` on the `ALS on landing` line. No report line starts with a state line's or a counted line's words (tests count those). It is 163 lines long in the fake game (a test holds it under 200), likely about 200 in the game, whose character has more cameras and meshes; the cameras' picture and lens lines are the place to cut. It only uses the controller the frame hook got.
- **Spreading the work.** The camera's 0.5 s refresh (pitch limits, hiding, shakes), the camera list's 0.5 s rescan and the movement's 0.5 s refresh fall on different frames: whenever first person starts (also on a new camera manager), the rescan is moved to 0.25 s and the movement's refresh to 0.125 s after the camera's, and when the movement starts again while first person is on, its refresh goes 0.125 s after one of the camera's. Each counts its next run from when the last was due, not from the frame it ran on, so uneven frame times don't bring them together. A rescan because the camera shown changed doesn't move them. A frame longer than 0.125 s, the first frame of first person and the frame the movement starts can still carry two of them.
- **Units and defaults.** Settings are in Half-Life units, with `UnitScale` 2.5 cm per unit. The defaults are Half-Life 1's cvars, auto hop is on, and `BhopCap` is off. As in Half-Life, the jump speed is fixed (`JumpHeight` is reached at `Gravity` 800), so less gravity jumps higher. The keys ask for `InputSpeed` (400, Half-Life's `cl_forwardspeed`, a setting `config.lua` doesn't list) and `MaxSpeed` caps that, so `MaxSpeed` above 400 changes nothing for running without a higher `InputSpeed`, as in Half-Life; `config.lua`'s comment and the README's row say so, and `docs/TESTING.md`'s slope boosting shortcut sets both to 800.
- **Reading config.lua.** `main.lua` runs the file with `loadfile` and globals of its own (`settings.environment`: Lua's globals, read through a table that notes every name that isn't set, with its line), at load and in the F7 key callback. Lua reads a word it doesn't know (`flase`, `False`, a name without its quotes) as a global that isn't set: nil, and a table entry set to nil doesn't exist, so such a setting would keep its default without a word and a list would end at that entry (`settings.load` walks lists with `ipairs`). So one warning names the words and their lines (the first 8, then `and N more`), right before F7's `Settings reloaded` line, whose `Changed:` part can't name such a setting; the list still ends there, and the warning says so. A file Lua can't read at all (a lost comma; the shipped file with its `return` deleted, which leaves a `{` that can't start a statement; one that lost its last line) gives the defaults and a warning with Lua's message. A file that runs and gives nothing (an empty file, blank lines or a byte order mark only, one with only comments, a bare `return`, a table kept in a local or a global and not returned) gives the defaults with the warning `Could not read config.lua, so the default settings are used: it gives no settings (it has to start its list with return {, as the shipped one does)`, at load and at F7. UE4SS's Lua is stock 5.4 with all its libraries (`luaL_openlibs`; of the base functions UE4SS only replaces `print`), so `loadfile` opens the file exactly as `dofile` did (a reviewer's reading of UE4SS's source, not tried in the game). `main.lua` finds its own folder with `debug.getinfo` (UE4SS names its chunk `@` and its path); without the `debug` library, or under a chunk name without `@`, it would read `config.lua` with `require("config")`, which notes no words, and F7 would get the table Lua already loaded instead of reading the file again (that branch can't run under UE4SS and was left without the check on purpose). `Speedometer`, `StartEnabled` and the hotkeys are only read when the mod loads. The test "the shipped config.lua loads without warnings" reads the file with the mod's globals, as the mod does (its text through `load`: `fake.useConfig` replaces the global `loadfile` for the rest of the run), so a slip in the shipped file fails it, whatever test files ran before.

## About Half-Life (from the owner's log of 2026-11-11, and from published descriptions)

- The owner's log. On 2026-11-11 the owner recorded a Bunnymod XT log in Half-Life itself (the 2005 WON build, engine build 3248, mod `valve_WON`; `fps_max 100`, `bxt_autojump 1`): 139 hops on and around one ramp of 17.7° and on the level floor at its foot, in 13,419 unpaused physics frames, 13,076 of them with an update of 10 ms, about 70 of 11 to 19 ms and a stretch of about 270 at 29 to 33 ms (about 31 fps). Each update in it has the player before and after the movement code (position, velocity, on the ground or not), the update's length in whole milliseconds, the buttons and the view. It is the only evidence from the real game: everything else about Half-Life's landings came from published descriptions. The log, and text from it, must never be copied into the repository (nothing taken from a player's PC); numbers measured from it may be quoted in comments, tests and docs.
- What it shows. For each landing take the update it happened in: the place before it, plus the vertical speed less half the update's gravity, times the update's length, is where a free move would have ended.
  - Every landing whose free move ended above where the player was put, by up to 2 units (the largest is 1.955; 1.986 above the floor's plane), left the velocity exactly as it was, on the ramp and on the level floor.
  - Every landing whose move reached the floor was a collision: on the ramp the horizontal speed changed in that one update (13 landings); on the level floor nothing changed, since there is nothing to clip (11).
  - On the spot on the ramp with every update 10 ms: 67 updates per hop, 0.220 units above every time, speed 0 before and after (a published figure is 66 updates in the air).
  - Down the ramp at 90.47 u/s: 74 updates per hop, 0.947 units above, and the speed stays for as many hops as the ramp lasts.
  - Ducked in the air on the spot: 73 updates, 0.254 units below, and 0 becomes 90.47 u/s in that update, five times of five. On the ramp the ducked box's plane lies 17.0 units below the standing one's, not 18.
  - With one to three updates of 11 to 13 ms in a hop on the spot: clipped in four cases (0 becomes 76.8 to 77.2 u/s), set down in three others. At about 31 fps three of four landings were clipped (0 to 78, 78 to 161, 161 to 250 u/s).
  - One landing going up: from the level floor onto the ramp at 498 u/s after 42 updates, 1.589 units above, speed kept. It is the log's fastest landing: nothing in it is fast enough for a slide (635 u/s on that ramp).
  - The player rests 1/32 unit above the floor's plane (at 36.03125 on the level floor, whose plane its collisions name at 36). The jump's own update ends rising 260.3 u/s (268.3 less one update of gravity) after moving with 264.3.
- So in Half-Life a landing is all or nothing, decided by where its last update ends. The gain at a clipped landing from a fall straight down is the fall speed times the sine times the cosine of the slope: about 268 × 0.290 = 77 u/s on that ramp, and 90 after the longer ducked fall.
- An update's length. Half-Life moves the player in whole milliseconds. In the log every update is whole and is the frame time cut down (all 227 frames half a millisecond or more past a whole one ran with it cut down), with nothing carried over (frame time less update time adds up to 273 ms over the log; BXT's remainder field reads 0 throughout). So in the 2005 version, as on the engines before about build 6027 (jwchong.com/hl, "Slowdown on older engines"), a frame rate where 1000 / rate isn't whole runs shorter updates and a slower player: at 144 fps 6 ms ones (more gain per hop than `PhysicsRate` 144); at 95 and 98 fps 10 ms and at 101 fps 9 ms, so a hop on the spot on a slope stays put; at 60, 72, 80 and 90 fps 16, 13, 12 and 11 ms, where it moves off. The 2005 version also holds the frame rate to 100 unless BXT's `bxt_remove_fps_limit` lifts it. Later engines, today's Steam version among them, carry the leftover to the next update (the frametime remainder in `CL_Move`, which BXT can set), so their updates mix two lengths that average the frame's (at 144 fps 7 ms, with a 6 ms one in 18): by the tests' model of Half-Life fed those lengths, `PhysicsRate` 144 gains within 0.3 u/s a hop of that, and a hop on the spot on 10° moves off at 80, 95, 98 and 101 as the mod's does (at 110 it moves off where the mod's stays put). That is from published descriptions, not from a log; a short `bxt_taslog` in the owner's Steam Half-Life at fps_max 144 would show it. `PhysicsRate` N is every version's fps_max N only where 1000 / N is whole (100, 125, 200, 250).
- Published descriptions the mod's rules come from (none is the Half-Life SDK, which this project doesn't read): kz-rush.com's physics articles ("EdgeBug & JumpBug physics" for the ground check's 2 units; the walk move's two tries), the Half-Life physics reference at jwchong.com/hl (a rise over 180 u/s is in the air; the ground check; the update's length), the Bunnymod XT wiki (its console commands), ryanliptak.com on ramp sliding, and Quake's and QuakeWorld's GPL source (the step move, the ground move that goes straight ahead, and the 1/32 unit, which the log shows Half-Life has too).
- The reviews compared the mod with reference simulations of Half-Life's updates written from those descriptions: they give all 138 of the log's landings they can fly, and the latest (the 0.3.1 final review's, with Half-Life's ground move, slide move and edge friction) reproduces the owner's log and demo update by update; the leaps down a slope, the slides and the strafing were compared with it.
- Ducking (0.4.0) comes from specs written from published descriptions, not from the Half-Life SDK or OpenAG's source: Half-Life's duck (three states, the 1000/600 ms timer, the view's curve, 0.333 of the moves fully ducked, a duck tap that lifts the player 18 units with no vertical speed, the duck step before the jump) and OpenAG's client for the duck button (+duck, a notch for one command, +ducktap with its memory and, from its build of May 2022, a duck key held under it). The owner plays OpenAG of May 2022 at 250 updates a second; his Adrenaline Gamer demo (a +ducktap session) and the Bunnymod XT log are what an acceptance replay outside the repository checks the mod's duck against, update by update. Valve's map compiler expands each brush for each hull, so on a slope the ducked hull's floor lies 18 nz units below the standing one's, rounded to whole units (the log's ramp: 17.0).
- What the mod doesn't have of Half-Life's movement: the long jump (ducking came in 0.4.0; 34 of the log's 139 hops are ducked, and a ducked hop on the spot on the ramp is clipped where a standing one isn't, as the mod's is at `PhysicsRate` 100), surfing, the extra friction near ledge edges, leaving the ground going down stairs, water and ladders.
